# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used across this site. Where a term has its own page, a link points there.

**Autosomal dominant** — An inheritance pattern in which a single altered copy of a gene (out of the two everyone has) is enough to cause a condition. PRRT2 conditions follow this pattern. See [Inheritance & Genetics](../overview/inheritance-and-genetics.md).

**BFIS (Benign Familial Infantile Seizures)** — Also called self-limited familial infantile epilepsy. Seizures beginning in infancy that typically resolve in early childhood with normal development. See [BFIS](../conditions/bfis.md).

**Basal ganglia** — Deep brain structures involved in controlling movement. Central to several PRRT2-related conditions.

**c.649dupC** — The single most common PRRT2 mutation, found in roughly 80% of patients. A duplication of one DNA letter that creates a premature stop, resulting in a non-working protein.

**Carbamazepine** — A sodium-channel-blocking medication; the established first-line treatment for PKD, often effective at low doses. See [Carbamazepine](../treatments/medications/carbamazepine.md).

**Cataplexy** — Sudden loss of muscle tone, usually triggered by emotion, classically associated with narcolepsy. Relevant to the discussion on [Sodium Oxybate](../treatments/medications/sodium-oxybate.md).

**Cerebellum** — A brain region important for coordinating movement; implicated in PRRT2-related dyskinesia in research models.

**Choreoathetosis** — A combination of irregular, dance-like movements (chorea) and slow, writhing movements (athetosis). Seen in some paroxysmal episodes.

**Dyskinesia** — Abnormal, involuntary movement. The "D" in PKD and PED.

**Dystonia** — Involuntary muscle contraction producing twisting movements or sustained abnormal postures. See [Dystonia](../conditions/dystonia.md).

**Dysphonia** — Disordered voice production. See [Dysphonia](../wider-spectrum/dysphonia.md).

**Haploinsufficiency** — When having only one working copy of a gene is not enough for normal function. The mechanism behind most PRRT2 conditions.

**Hemiplegic migraine** — A severe migraine with temporary one-sided weakness as part of the aura. See [Hemiplegic Migraine](../conditions/hemiplegic-migraine.md).

**Heterozygous** — Having one altered and one normal copy of a gene. The usual situation in PRRT2 conditions.

**Homozygous / compound heterozygous** — Having both copies of a gene altered. Rare in PRRT2, and associated with more severe presentations.

**ICCA (Infantile Convulsions with Choreoathetosis)** — The combination of infantile seizures (BFIS) and later movement episodes (PKD) in the same person or family.

**Kinesigenic** — Triggered by movement. The "K" in PKD; episodes are set off by sudden voluntary movement.

**Lacosamide** — A newer sodium-channel medication used as an alternative in some paroxysmal conditions. See [Lacosamide](../treatments/medications/lacosamide.md).

**MSLT (Multiple Sleep Latency Test)** — A daytime sleep study used in evaluating narcolepsy and related conditions.

**Paroxysmal** — Occurring in sudden, distinct episodes with normal function in between. A defining feature of most PRRT2 conditions.

**Pathogenic variant** — A genetic change established to cause disease. A pathogenic PRRT2 variant confirms the diagnosis. See [Understanding Your Results](../diagnosis/understanding-your-results.md).

**PED (Paroxysmal Exertional Dyskinesia)** — Movement episodes triggered by sustained exertion. See [PED](../conditions/ped.md).

**Penetrance** — The proportion of people with a gene variant who actually show symptoms. PRRT2 has incomplete penetrance — some carriers never develop symptoms.

**PKD (Paroxysmal Kinesigenic Dyskinesia)** — Brief movement episodes triggered by sudden movement; the most common and most treatable PRRT2 condition. See [PKD](../conditions/pkd.md).

**Polysomnography (PSG)** — An overnight sleep study recording brain activity, breathing, and movement.

**PRRT2** — Proline-Rich Transmembrane Protein 2; the gene (on chromosome 16p11.2) at the center of this site. See [Introduction to PRRT2](../overview/introduction.md).

**SNARE complex / SNAP-25** — The molecular machinery that controls neurotransmitter release at synapses. PRRT2 interacts with it. See [How the Gene Works](../overview/how-the-gene-works.md).

**Sodium channel** — A protein that lets sodium ions into neurons, central to electrical signaling. PRRT2 helps regulate neuronal excitability connected to these channels. See [The Sodium Channel Connection](../overview/sodium-channel-connection.md).

**Sodium oxybate** — A medication established for narcolepsy with cataplexy; exploratory in relation to PRRT2. See [Sodium Oxybate](../treatments/medications/sodium-oxybate.md).

**Spasmodic dysphonia** — A focal dystonia of the larynx affecting the voice. Treatable with botulinum toxin.

**Variable expressivity** — When the same gene variant produces different symptoms or severities in different people. A hallmark of PRRT2.

**VUS (Variant of Uncertain Significance)** — A genetic change whose effect is not yet classifiable as disease-causing or benign. See [Understanding Your Results](../diagnosis/understanding-your-results.md).

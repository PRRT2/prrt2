# Links & Support

The [Official Resources](official-resources.md) page collects the authoritative _scientific_ references for PRRT2. This page is different — it is about **support and connection**: the people, communities, and organizations that help with living through a PRRT2 diagnosis, not just understanding it.

## Our community

* **PRRT2 Gene Alliance Facebook group** — the heart of our community, where people with PRRT2 and their families connect, compare experiences, and support one another. _(_[_Groups PRRT2_](https://facebook.com/groups/prrt2)_)_
* **PRRT2.org** — this knowledge base. If it has helped you, sharing it with others — patients, families, even clinicians — is one of the simplest ways to extend its reach.

## Finding the right specialists

As emphasized throughout this site, the right specialist makes an enormous difference. These directories can help you find one:

* **International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society** — a directory of movement-disorder specialists worldwide.
* **National Society of Genetic Counselors** — find a certified genetic counselor at [findageneticcounselor.com](https://findageneticcounselor.com).

(See [How to Get Tested](../diagnosis/how-to-get-tested.md) for more on choosing the right kind of clinician.)

## Rare-disease and condition organizations

These organizations offer information, support, and sometimes help finding care:

* **NORD (National Organization for Rare Disorders)** — [rarediseases.org](https://rarediseases.org) — information and patient-support programs for rare conditions.
* **Dystonia Medical Research Foundation** — support and information for the dystonia side of the spectrum.
* **Epilepsy support organizations** — relevant for the seizure-related conditions; national epilepsy foundations offer practical and emotional support.
* **For voice symptoms** — the NIH NIDCD provides reliable information on [spasmodic dysphonia](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/spasmodic-dysphonia); voice-disorder support communities also exist.
* **For tic symptoms** — the Tourette Association of America ([tourette.org](https://tourette.org)) offers community and resources.

## Practical and emotional support

Living with a chronic, paroxysmal condition has a real emotional dimension, and support for that is legitimate care:

* **Peer support** — connecting with others who understand (starting with our Facebook group) is, for many people, the single most validating thing after diagnosis.
* **Mental health support** — counseling or therapy can help with the weight of an unpredictable condition and the long road many travel to diagnosis.
* **Caregiver support** — if you care for someone with PRRT2, your wellbeing matters too. See [For Caregivers](../living/for-caregivers.md).

**🧭 PRRT2.org Perspective**

_PRRT2.org's own perspective — our synthesis and lived experience, offered alongside the established science, not as established medical fact._

For a rare condition, connection is not a soft extra — it is often where the most useful information lives. The clinician who finally understood your episodes, the trick that helped with a trigger, the reassurance that you are not imagining things: these are passed person to person, in communities like ours.

PRRT2.org's hope is to grow into the kind of organization that does more than inform — that connects, supports, and eventually helps fund the research this community deserves. That growth starts with people finding each other.

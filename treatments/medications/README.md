# Medications

This subsection covers the medications used in PRRT2-related conditions, one page each. They fall into a few groups:

* **Sodium-channel modulators** — the mainstay for the paroxysmal movement and seizure conditions. [Carbamazepine](carbamazepine.md) is the best established; [Lacosamide](lacosamide.md) is a newer option in the same broad family. Several related agents appear in [Other Medications](other-medications.md).
* **Targeted, localized treatment** — [Botulinum Toxin](botulinum-toxin.md), used for focal dystonia and for laryngeal voice disorders, acting only where it is injected.
* **Emerging and exploratory options** — [Sodium Oxybate](sodium-oxybate.md), established for narcolepsy with cataplexy, and of interest to part of the PRRT2 community for reasons we lay out honestly on that page.

> **Not a prescribing guide.** These pages describe what each medication is and the role it plays. They do not give doses, and they are not a substitute for a clinician's judgment. Every medication here has potential side effects and interactions that must be weighed by a prescriber who knows your full situation.

## How to read these pages

Where a medication has an established role in a PRRT2-related condition, we say so plainly and cite it. Where its relevance to PRRT2 is emerging or exploratory, we label it clearly. The distinction matters most for [Sodium Oxybate](sodium-oxybate.md), which is well established for a different condition and not an established PRRT2 treatment.

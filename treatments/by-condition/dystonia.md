# Treatment for Dystonia

How dystonia is treated in the PRRT2 context depends on **what kind of dystonia it is** — the brief, paroxysmal dystonia of PKD, or a more focal, sustained dystonic problem. The two call for different approaches.

## Paroxysmal dystonia (as part of PKD)

When dystonia appears as part of the brief, movement-triggered episodes of PKD — the limb twisting and stiffening during an episode, then returning to normal — it is treated as PKD. That means a **sodium-channel medication**, first-line [carbamazepine](../medications/carbamazepine.md), which controls the episodes themselves and therefore the dystonia within them. (See [Treatment for PKD](pkd.md).)

This is the most common dystonia picture in PRRT2, and it responds to the same well-established treatment that controls PKD.

## Focal, sustained dystonia

When a specific muscle group is persistently dystonic — rather than dystonia coming and going in brief episodes — the approach shifts toward **targeted, local treatment**:

* **[Botulinum toxin](../medications/botulinum-toxin.md)** is the mainstay for focal dystonia. Injected into the overactive muscle, it relieves that specific area for a period of months, after which it is repeated.
* **Oral medications** are sometimes used for dystonia more broadly, with variable benefit, and are weighed individually by a specialist.
* **Physical and occupational therapy** can support function and comfort alongside medical treatment (see [Therapies](../therapies.md)).

## A note on the wider picture

As discussed in [Dystonia](../../conditions/dystonia.md), persistent or generalized dystonia is not classically described as a core PRRT2 phenotype, even though PRRT2 sits on dystonia gene panels. For people whose dystonic experience extends beyond the paroxysmal pattern, treatment is individualized, and the underlying cause is worth clarifying with a movement-disorder specialist.

> Informational only — not medical advice. Dystonia treatment, and botulinum toxin in particular, must be directed and administered by a qualified specialist.

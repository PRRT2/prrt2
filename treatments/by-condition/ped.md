# Treatment for PED

Treatment for paroxysmal exertional dyskinesia depends heavily on **which gene is behind it** — which is why getting the genetic picture right matters so much for this condition in particular.

## First, the underlying cause shapes everything

PED can be caused by different genes, and they call for different strategies:

* **SLC2A1 (GLUT1 deficiency)** — the most common cause of PED. Here, the **ketogenic diet** is often the central, and frequently very effective, treatment. The diet provides the brain with an alternative fuel that bypasses the underlying glucose-transport problem. This is a medically supervised diet, not a casual one.
* **PRRT2** — a less common cause of PED. Where PRRT2 is responsible, the **sodium-channel medications** used for PKD (such as [carbamazepine](../medications/carbamazepine.md)) may help — though the response in exertional forms is generally **less reliable and less dramatic** than it is in classic kinesigenic PKD.

Because the treatments diverge so sharply, **establishing the underlying gene is part of the treatment decision**, not just an academic question. (See [How to Get Tested](../../diagnosis/how-to-get-tested.md).)

## Managing the exertional trigger

Whatever the cause, the trigger for PED is **sustained exertion**. Practical management includes pacing activity, recognizing the threshold at which episodes tend to begin, and — particularly in GLUT1-related PED — attention to fuel and timing around exertion. These measures are discussed in [Lifestyle & Diet](../lifestyle-and-diet.md).

> Informational only — not a prescribing or dietary guide. The ketogenic diet in particular requires medical supervision, and all treatment decisions belong to a specialist.

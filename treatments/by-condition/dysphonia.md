# Treatment for Dysphonia

An important point first: as explained in [Dysphonia](../../wider-spectrum/dysphonia.md), a connection between PRRT2 and dysphonia is **not established**. But the good news for anyone living with voice symptoms is that **the voice disorder can usually be evaluated and treated on its own terms** — independently of whether a PRRT2 link is ever proven.

## Get the voice disorder properly diagnosed first

Effective treatment starts with knowing what kind of dysphonia is present. A **laryngologist** (an ENT specialist focused on the voice) or a **movement-disorder specialist** can determine whether the voice problem is, for example, spasmodic dysphonia (a focal dystonia of the larynx), a muscle-tension pattern, or something else. The treatment depends entirely on that answer.

## If it is spasmodic dysphonia

For spasmodic dysphonia specifically, treatment is well established:

* **[Botulinum toxin](../medications/botulinum-toxin.md)** injected into the laryngeal muscles is the **standard of care**. It relieves the abnormal muscle activity that disrupts the voice, for a period of months at a time, and is then repeated. This is delicate, specialized work best done by an experienced injector.
* **Voice (speech-language) therapy** is often used alongside injections to get the most functional benefit (see [Therapies](../therapies.md)).

## If it is another form of dysphonia

Other voice disorders have their own established treatments — voice therapy, behavioral techniques, and management of any contributing factors. A laryngologist and a speech-language pathologist guide this. The key message is the same: **the voice problem deserves direct evaluation and treatment**, whatever its relationship to PRRT2 turns out to be.

## The PRRT2 connection — kept honest

If you carry a PRRT2 variant and have voice symptoms, documenting them carefully (see [Symptom Tracking](../../diagnosis/symptom-tracking.md)) helps both your own care and the broader, still-open question of whether voice involvement belongs in the PRRT2 picture. But you do not need to wait for that question to be settled to get help with your voice now.

> Informational only — not medical advice. Voice-disorder treatment, and laryngeal botulinum toxin in particular, must be directed by a qualified specialist.

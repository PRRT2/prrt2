# Treatment for BFIS

Benign familial infantile seizures are, as the name promises, generally a **good-outcome condition**. Treatment reflects that: the goal is to control seizures while they are occurring, with the reassurance that most children outgrow them.

## The general approach

* **Anti-seizure medication when needed.** When treatment is used, sodium-channel agents — **[carbamazepine](../medications/carbamazepine.md)** and related medications — are commonly effective. Seizures typically respond well.
* **A self-limiting course.** Many infants outgrow the seizures within the first few years of life, often by around age two or three. Because of this, the duration of treatment is usually limited, and decisions about stopping medication are made by the treating clinician as the child develops.
* **Normal development is the expectation.** BFIS does not typically affect cognitive or developmental outcomes — part of what makes it "benign."

## What matters most for families

For BFIS, the medical management is often the straightforward part. The more lasting value frequently comes from **recognition** — understanding that the infantile seizures may be one expression of an inherited PRRT2 variant, so that if a movement disorder or migraine appears later in the same child or in a relative, the connection is already understood. (See [Benign Familial Infantile Seizures](../../conditions/bfis.md) and [Inheritance & Genetics](../../overview/inheritance-and-genetics.md).)

> Informational only. Treatment of infantile seizures must always be directed by a pediatric neurologist or epilepsy specialist.

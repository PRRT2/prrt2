# Patient Stories

Behind every line of clinical description on this site is a person who lived it. This page is for their stories — the lived experience of PRRT2, in the words of the people who carry it and the families who love them.

## Why stories matter here

For a condition as under-recognized as PRRT2, stories are not decoration. They do real work:

* They **make the invisible visible** — showing the lived texture of symptoms that a clinical summary flattens into a list.
* They **reach people who are still undiagnosed** — someone reading a story may recognize their own decades of unexplained episodes and finally have a word for them.
* They **document the wider reality** — the adult experience, the long-undiagnosed years, the symptoms outside the textbook — that the formal literature has been slow to capture.
* They **remind everyone that no one is alone with this** — which, for a rare condition, is itself a kind of medicine.

In that sense, lived experience is part of this site's evidence base, sitting honestly alongside the science rather than pretending to be it.

## A founder's story

This knowledge base grew out of one person's path to a PRRT2 diagnosis — decades of unexplained symptoms, a diagnosis that finally arrived in adulthood through a movement-disorder gene panel, and the discovery that the public information about the gene barely reflected the reality of living with it. That story is told on the **[About — The Reality of PRRT2](../about.md)** page, and it is the reason this site exists.

## Share your story

This page grows with our community. If you carry a PRRT2 variant, care for someone who does, or have lived the long road to diagnosis, **your story is welcome here** — in whatever form you are comfortable sharing it.

You can connect with the community and share your experience through the **PRRT2 Foundation Facebook group** _(link on the [Links & Support](../resources/links-and-support.md) page)_. Stories shared there, with permission, can help shape and populate this page over time.

Share as much or as little as you like. What helped you get diagnosed, what you wish someone had told you sooner, what living with PRRT2 actually feels like — all of it matters to someone who is where you once were.
